THE IDEA

Magic in Wyrd is a written language. Your opponent writes a rune in the air, stroke by stroke. You read it while it is still being written and draw the rune that undoes it before it lands.

Reading is the skill. The earlier you read their rune, the more your answer is charged. Read early enough and you have time to chain two runes into a combo. Drawing cleanly adds power. Guessing wrong costs you, but a misread can be corrected while their rune is still in the air.

AN EXCHANGE (RUNE FENCING)

  1. They write. Their rune grows over three seconds, big above the arena and in the air by their hand. Every rune begins with the same flat opening; the read goes from FLAT OPENING to the first sign of its shape to its name.
  2. You ready a counter. Draw a rune and lift your finger: it is readied, not fired. The readout shows it and its charge.
  3. Adjust or combo. Drawing again replaces your answer, which restarts its charge, or finishes a combo if the pair is one.
  4. Impact. Their rune flies and lands. Your readied rune meets it. A counter hurts them, more the earlier you readied it (up to +60%). A wrong rune hurts you less the cleaner it was drawn. No rune takes the full blow.

First to empty the other's HP wins. When the minute runs out, the healthier mage wins.

READING THEIR RUNE

They write every rune from the same opening: a flat stroke from the left (white). Then it shows itself:

  • ⌒ Redirect bows up, ⌣ Absorb bows down: the earliest to show, about a quarter of the way.
  • ○ Ward runs a little further, then curls back into a loop.
  • △ Power stays flat along its whole base, then turns at a sharp corner.
  • — Pierce never turns: you only know it once it has stayed flat past where Power would have cornered.

You do not need the whole rune to answer. Pierce and Power are undone by the same runes (Ward, Redirect), and so are Redirect and Absorb (Power, Pierce). "Still flat" already tells you a Ward or a Redirect will do; "it bowed" says Power or Pierce. Wait for the name only when you want the combo that needs it.

You draw your own runes the usual way (see How to draw); only theirs share the opening.

COMBOS

Ready a starter, then draw the finisher before impact. The finisher must still undo their rune. A combo keeps the starter's charge, so build it early.

  • EMPOWER · — Pierce then △ Power: aim, then drive. The counter lands half again as hard.
  • REFLECT · ○ Ward then ⌒ Redirect: their blow is turned back on them as extra damage.
  • SIPHON · ⌣ Absorb then — Pierce: you heal as much as you deal.

Every rune they write can be answered by at least one combo's finisher.

HOW TO DRAW

One stroke per rune, in the panel at the bottom of the arena. Recognition is forgiving; a cleaner stroke hits harder.

— PierceOne confident straight stroke left → right. Keep it long and avoid hooks.
⌒ RedirectOne smooth upward arc. Keep both ends open and the middle clearly above them.
○ WardOne closed loop. Finish close to the starting point; closure matters more than perfect roundness.
△ PowerThree deliberate sides in one stroke. Make three clear corners and close the triangle.
⌣ AbsorbOne smooth downward arc — the inverse of Redirect. Keep the middle clearly below the two ends.

Ward and Power both close on themselves; Power is told apart by its three corners, so make them sharp.

THE TWO MODES

Rune Fencing: the real-time duel above, exchange after exchange for a minute.

Rune Parry: you glimpse only the opening of their stroke (about a third) and must commit your rune within four seconds; then both are revealed. A bow up or down may already show in the glimpse; a stroke still flat could be Pierce, Power or a Ward about to curl. Pick the rune that answers the most of what it could be.

CONTROLS

Speed slows or quickens everything, the minute included. View changes the camera: drag the arena to rotate, pinch or ± to zoom. Log lists the duel from the first exchange down to the latest. Reset starts the mode again.
